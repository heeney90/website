Money management

Where an emergency fund should sit

November 1, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

An emergency fund is money you can use in a few days without selling an investment or adding a new debt. If the only backup is a credit card, the emergency becomes a balance.

Why this matters now

Housing costs in Ontario leave less slack than a textbook budget assumes. The fund does not have to be six months of income on day one. It has to be real, separate, and boring.

A practical checklist

  • Name a first target you can finish: one month of must-pay bills is a stronger start than a number you will not reach.
  • Keep it in a savings account you do not see on the debit card you use for groceries.
  • Refill it after you use it, before you increase extra debt payments.
  • Do not invest the first month of reserves. Market risk is the wrong risk for a furnace or a job gap.
  • If income is variable, lean toward a larger cash buffer before aggressive investing or extra mortgage prepayments.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a line of credit an emergency fund. It is a borrowing facility, and it can be reduced or frozen.
  • Draining the cash to make a down payment with nothing left for the first repair.
  • Stopping at the idea of a fund and never moving the first transfer.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

Cash reserves show up in real life more often than they show up as a rate. A purchase or refinance that leaves the household with no cash is fragile, even when the payment qualifies. Say that out loud before you stretch the down payment.
