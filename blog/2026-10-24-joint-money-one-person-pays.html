Money management

Joint money when one person pays the bills

October 24, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

One person often runs the payments because they are better at it, or because they always have. The trouble starts when the other person meets the mortgage, the cards, or a refinance for the first time at the lender’s desk.

Why this matters now

A purchase or a refinance needs both people if both are on the application. Surprises in that meeting are usually old information that only one partner had been watching.

A practical checklist

  • Once a month, sit down for the money meeting even if one person still clicks the payments.
  • Look at the same four lines: income in, housing out, other debt, and what is left.
  • Give both people access to see the accounts, even if only one person moves the money.
  • Agree on a spending cap each person can use without a debate, and a number that requires a conversation.
  • Before any application, both people should be able to explain the debts on the form.

Common mistakes

  • Hiding a card to keep the peace, then disclosing it when a lender pulls the report.
  • Treating “you handle it” as “I do not need to know.”
  • Having the money talk only when something has already gone wrong.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

Come to the meeting together if you will both be on the mortgage. The numbers are easier to hear from a page you already share than from a condition you did not expect.
