A consolidation loan or a refinance: which question to ask first

October 26, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

A consolidation loan leaves the mortgage alone and puts several balances into one unsecured payment. A refinance rewrites the mortgage and can absorb those balances into the house. They solve different problems. Ads treat them as the same product.

Why this matters now

The offer that arrives first is usually the one with the friendliest monthly number. The first question is which debts you are trying to finish, and whether you are willing to secure them with the home.

A practical checklist

  • If you can qualify for a personal loan that clears the cards in a few years, price that before you touch the mortgage.
  • If the cards are large relative to your income, or a renewal is already forcing a new mortgage decision, price the refinance as well.
  • Compare total interest and the date the debt is gone, not only the new payment.
  • Ask what happens if spending on the cards continues. A loan you can refill is not a consolidation.
  • Ask about penalties on the current mortgage before assuming a refinance is available this month.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the lowest payment with the longest life.
  • Ignoring a prepayment penalty that wipes out the supposed savings.
  • Leaving a high-interest card open with the same limit and no rule for it.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

Mark can lay the refinance beside the unsecured option when a mortgage change is actually available. If the current term has a penalty that makes a refinance a poor trade, he should say that. Availability depends on the lender, the property, and the file.
