The 50/30/20 rule when the mortgage already takes half

October 27, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

The 50/30/20 split says about half of take-home pay goes to needs, 30 percent to wants, and 20 percent to saving and extra debt payments. In a lot of Ontario households the mortgage, tax, heat, and groceries already consume the first half. Pretending the textbook still fits does not make the month work.

Why this matters now

People use the rule to feel behind. A rule is a measuring stick. If housing is the reason the stick does not fit, the next step is to see the real split, not to shame the grocery bill.

A practical checklist

  • Calculate the split from take-home pay for one ordinary month.
  • Put housing, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments, and basic food in needs.
  • If needs are over half, do not invent a 20 percent savings rate you cannot fund. Name the actual remainder.
  • Cut wants only after you have seen them. Many “needs” are ordinary spending with no receipt.
  • If needs stay above what the income can carry, the payment or the debt load is the issue. Budget categories will not close that gap.

Common mistakes

  • Forcing 20 percent savings by putting the shortfall on a card.
  • Calling every shortfall a housing problem when subscriptions and car payments are doing the damage.
  • Comparing your split to a household with a paid-off house.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

If a renewal or a refinance would push needs further past what the month can hold, look at the structure before you accept the offer in the mailbox. A lower payment that adds years of interest still has to be priced. A payment that does not fit should not be signed just because the rule was already broken.
