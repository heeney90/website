Budgeting

A budget that can survive a variable paycheque

November 4, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

A budget built on a best month will fail in an average one. Commission, overtime, self-employment, and seasonal work need a floor: the amount that still shows up when the month is ordinary, not the amount that showed up once.

Why this matters now

Lenders and households both get into trouble when a strong month is treated as the new normal. A budget that uses the lower, repeatable income keeps the mortgage, groceries, and minimum debt payments inside money that is actually expected.

A practical checklist

  • Look at the last six deposits, not the best one. Circle the number you would still bet on.
  • Fund fixed bills from that floor: housing, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments, and a grocery base.
  • Park anything above the floor in a separate account until the month is closed. Then assign it: tax, emergency cash, or extra debt payment.
  • If you are self-employed, set aside a tax slice before the extra money feels spendable.
  • Rewrite the floor if three ordinary months in a row come in lower. Do not wait for a crisis.

Common mistakes

  • Averaging a boom month with a quiet month and then spending the average.
  • Raising lifestyle in the same week a large deposit lands.
  • Using a credit card to smooth a slow month and never naming a date to pay it back.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

Variable income can still support a mortgage, but the story has to match the documents. Bring the same floor you budget with. A payment sized to a peak month is a payment the quiet month cannot keep.
