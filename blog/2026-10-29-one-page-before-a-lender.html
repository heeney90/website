One page to fill out before you talk to a lender

October 29, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

A first call goes better when the numbers are on one page. You do not need a perfect application. You need the facts a mortgage agent would otherwise spend the meeting hunting for.

Why this matters now

People book a meeting when a purchase, a renewal, or a pile of payments has a date on it. The page does not approve you. It stops the conversation from being a guess.

A practical checklist

  • Income: who earns it, how you are paid, and what the last two years of tax documents roughly show.
  • Debts: every card, loan, and line of credit, with the balance, the payment, and the limit.
  • Housing: current payment, property tax, and what you still owe if you own.
  • The goal: buy, renew, refinance, or pull equity, and the date that matters.
  • Cash you could use without emptying the emergency fund, and any amount a family member has offered. Write whether that help is a gift.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out a card because the balance is “almost gone.”
  • Guessing income from a single pay stub when the year is uneven.
  • Waiting to gather documents until a condition deadline is already short.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

Bring the page. Mark can tell you what a lender is likely to ask next, and which goal does not fit the numbers yet. That is more useful than a rate quoted before the file exists.
